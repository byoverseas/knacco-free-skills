# Ethics and limits

A lead magnet speaks for a business before the business has said a word. Four things follow: what the result may claim, what the gate may ask, what the tool may collect, and what this product will not do for you.

## What a lead magnet may claim

A claim is safe when the tool can produce it from the user's own input, in front of them, and show the arithmetic.

| Allowed | Why it holds | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A computed figure from the user's numbers | The user can check it by hand | "At 12 missed appointments a week at 45 pounds, the month is 2,338 pounds short" |
| A stated rule with its date | It can be verified and it expires visibly | "Based on the 2025/26 threshold shown in the note under the table" |
| A comparison between the user's number and a lower number | It is arithmetic, labelled as arithmetic | "The same calculation at a miss rate one fifth lower gives 1,870 pounds" |
| A description of what the paid offer does | It is a statement about your own service, not about the user's outcome | "The review looks at the diary, the recall list and the reception script" |

## What a lead magnet must not claim

Four prohibitions. Each one is a specific sentence that a specific kind of tool tends to produce.

**1. A result it cannot compute.** "Find out how much you will earn in your first year" is not computable from four fields, and any number the tool returns is a guess wearing a suit. The rewrite: compute what the inputs actually support, and say what the number is. If the honest output is "your current monthly loss", the magnet is about the loss, not about the future.

**2. A guarantee.** "Guaranteed to cut no-shows by a third" is a promise about the future behaviour of other people. A tool cannot make it, and the buyer will hold the business to it. The rewrite: state the arithmetic and label the comparison as arithmetic. Never use the words guarantee, guaranteed, risk-free, proven or 100% in the promise field, and the checker will warn you if you do.

**3. A diagnosis.** A tool that tells a user what is wrong with them, their body, their business's legal position or their tax affairs is giving regulated advice with no one accountable for it. This is why the worked example counts appointments and multiplies money, and asks nothing about symptoms. If the offer is clinical, legal or financial advice, the magnet stops at counting and at describing the process.

**4. A number it did not calculate from the user's own input.** Averages presented as the user's figure, benchmarks presented as a result, and "typical" numbers presented as a finding. An average is a fact about a group, not a result for a person. The rewrite: show the benchmark as a benchmark, beside the user's own number, with both labelled.

There is a fifth rule that is not a claim but behaves like one: do not present an estimate with more precision than the inputs deserve. Four numbers typed from memory do not support two decimal places.

## Consent rules for the email gate

The gate asks for an address in exchange for the rest of a result. That exchange must be visible at the moment it happens.

1. **Say what the address is for, in the same screen as the field.** One sentence: what will be sent, how often, and that it can be stopped.
2. **Say what is behind the gate before asking.** "The annual figure and the lower-rate comparison are one step away" is honest. A field labelled "Get your results" over a result the user has already seen is not.
3. **No pre-ticked boxes.** Consent is an affirmative action. A box that arrives ticked is not consent, and a box that must be unticked to decline is worse.
4. **No bundling.** Agreeing to receive email is not the same as agreeing to terms of service, and it is not a condition of using a free calculator. If the result is genuinely free, the address is the price and the user must be able to see the price before paying it.
5. **Keep the record.** Store the date, the wording the user agreed to, and the page it happened on. Without that record, a complaint cannot be answered.
6. **Honour withdrawal without friction.** Every email carries a working way to stop. Stopping must not require an email to support or a login.
7. **Confirm where the audience expects it.** If your list is built in a jurisdiction or an industry where confirmed opt-in is the norm, confirm the address before sending anything else.
8. **Name the sender.** The email comes from a named business, with a real address, not from "the team".

None of this is legal advice, and the rules differ by country and by trade. The design rule underneath all eight is simpler than the law: the visitor must be able to see the trade before they make it.

## Data the tool must not collect

The calculation needs a handful of numbers. Anything beyond them is a liability with no upside.

| Never collect | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Health, symptom or treatment information | Special category data in most regimes, and a magnet has no clinical purpose |
| Bank details, card numbers, account numbers | The tool computes an estimate; it never needs to move money |
| Identity documents, dates of birth, addresses | Nothing in the arithmetic uses them |
| Children's data, or data from a tool aimed at children | Consent, safeguarding and purpose all fail at once |
| Passwords or login credentials for another service | The tool never needs to act as the user |
| Free-text boxes asking the user to describe their situation | Unbounded, unmoderated, and it will collect health and legal detail within a week |

Two further rules about the inputs themselves.

- **Do not silently capture what the user types.** If the numbers entered are sent to analytics, that must be said on the page. The cleanest position is that the arithmetic runs in the browser and the inputs are never stored at all.
- **Do not keep the inputs after the session.** The email address is the only thing that needs to persist. A calculation that leaves no record of the clinic's fee and no-show rate is easier to defend and easier to explain.

## Limits of this product

- It decides and specifies. It does not build the app, host it, send the emails or run the list.
- It does not verify your claims for you. The checker warns about claim words in the promise field; it cannot tell whether a sentence is true.
- It does not replace a lawyer, an accountant or a clinician where the trade needs one. Where the magnet touches a regulated area, the method's answer is to stop at counting and hand the judgement to a person.
- It does not guarantee that a lead magnet will produce leads. Nothing here reports traffic, conversion or revenue, and the success measure is a target written down before launch, not a result.
- It does not choose your weights for you forever. The six weights are a starting position with reasons attached, and a reader is expected to argue with them.
