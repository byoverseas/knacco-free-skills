# Question tests

Six tests. A question passes all six or it is not a study yet. Run them in order, because test 1 fails more questions than the other five combined and costs nothing to apply.

Do this before you open a browser tab, and before you ask anyone for data. The most expensive mistake in this workflow is collecting for a week and discovering that the question had no number in it.

---

## The checklist

| # | Test | The question you ask yourself | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The answer is a number | Can I write the answer as a count, a share, a median or a range? | You can write it as a sentence ending in a figure |
| 2 | The group is already covered | Which reporter has written about this group in the last year? | You can name the group and a publication that covers it |
| 3 | The answer could surprise | What do I expect, and could I be wrong? | Both possible answers are publishable, and one of them is not obvious |
| 4 | The property is visible in the row | Can I see the thing I am counting, in a line of text or a field, without inference? | A stranger could apply your rule to a row and agree |
| 5 | The population can be named and bounded | What exactly is the frame, and how many rows are in it? | You can state a number for the population before you start |
| 6 | You are not the subject | Is the answer about the reader, or about me? | The subject is not your own product, page or claim |

A question that fails 4 and 5 is a survey waiting to happen. A question that fails 6 is an advertisement.

---

## Worked pass

**Question:** Of 300 adverts for the same job title on one board, how many state a salary or a salary range?

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | The answer is a count: 118 of 300, or 39.3% of 300. Passes |
| 2 | Trade press, recruitment press and the business desk all cover pay transparency. Passes |
| 3 | Expectation was about half. Half would still be a story, and a third would be a bigger one. Passes |
| 4 | The salary line is either in the advert or it is not. No inference needed. Passes |
| 5 | One board, one search, one date. The population is the number of results on that date, recorded before filtering. Passes |
| 6 | The subject is the adverts, not the person asking. Passes |

**The study sentence:** *118 of the 300 adverts for this role on one board (39.3%) state no salary or range.*

**What is still missing at this point:** the counting rule. "States a salary" needs a positive test and a negative test, because "competitive" is not a number and "£40k plus benefits" is. That belongs to stage 4, and you should write it before you collect, not after.

## Worked fail

**Question:** Are small makers bad at telling buyers how to get their work?

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fails. "Bad at" is a judgement. There is no figure at the end of the sentence |
| 2 | Passes. Small makers are covered |
| 3 | Fails. The framing implies the answer before the count |
| 4 | Fails. "Bad at" is not visible in a row. A row can say nothing about delivery; it cannot say "bad" |
| 5 | Fails. "Small makers" has no frame: which directory, which region, which date |
| 6 | Passes, barely. The subject is makers, not the writer, though the tone suggests a complaint |

Three tests failed and one is at risk. Do not collect.

### The salvage move

Take the judgement out and put a property in. Ask what the rows can show:

> Of the 120 maker listings in one directory, how many state a way for a buyer to receive the item?

Now test 1 passes (a count), test 4 passes (either the words are there or they are not), test 5 passes (one directory, one date, 120 rows), and test 3 passes because the answer could land anywhere between a few and most.

That is the whole move: **replace the verdict with a property**. It is also the honest move, because you never had evidence for the verdict, and now you have evidence for something smaller and true.

## Shapes that always fail

These are recognisable, and each one fails for a reason that does not change with a better dataset.

| Shape | Example | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| The opinion | Are councils wasting money on consultants? | No count. Any figure becomes a verdict on people |
| The publisher | Is our platform the best place to sell craft work? | The answer is set by who is asking |
| The known answer | Do customers like free delivery? | No editor will run it. The count becomes decoration |
| The topic | The state of UK manufacturing | A subject, not a question. Nothing to count |
| The single case | One firm's experience of late payments | A story, not a study. One row is not a population |
| The forecast | How many makers will close next year? | Needs a model, not a count. A count cannot see the future |
| The comparison with no frame | Are makers happier than they were? | Two populations, two rules, no stable measure, and "happier" is not in a row |
| The uncollectable | How many makers feel ignored by buyers? | Feelings need a survey with a sampling design. Counting listings cannot reach them |
| The vanity | How many people downloaded our report? | It is about the publisher, and the denominator is your own server log |
| The proxy | How many makers are struggling, judged by how short their listings are? | The row does not contain the thing being claimed. This is inference wearing a count's coat |

## The two minute pre-flight

Before you collect, write these five lines. If any line is blank, you are not ready.

```
Question:
The answer will be a number like:
The frame is:
Journalists already write about this group because:
The most surprising answer would be:
```

Keep the five lines. They become the opening of the method note and the first paragraph of the findings, and they are the record of what you expected before you looked. Being wrong about the expectation is not a problem. Not writing it down is, because then nobody can tell whether the finding surprised you or was arranged.

---

## When the tests pass but the question is still weak

Two more things are worth checking, and neither is a hard test.

- **Is the frame too small to matter?** 40 rows from one street is a count, and it is not a story. Say what the frame is and let the reader judge, but do not call it national.
- **Is the answer already published?** If a body publishes this figure annually, your count needs a new angle, a new frame or a new date. Otherwise you are re-reporting someone else's number with worse methodology.
